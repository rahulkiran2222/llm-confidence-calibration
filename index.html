04 / Method

How confidence was estimated

Model-derived probabilities over answer choices—not verbal self-reported confidence.

STEP 01

Sample the benchmark

Up to 100 MMLU questions per subject: 70 development and 30 held-out for each of three domains.

STEP 02

Score four options

Conditional sequence log-likelihood scores for A–D are transformed into a relative probability distribution using softmax.

STEP 03

Evaluate calibration

The highest-probability option is the prediction. Its probability is compared with empirical accuracy using five fixed bins and ECE.

Research question

Does FLAN-T5-base's predictive confidence align with empirical accuracy, and does calibration differ across biology, computer science, and statistics?

Registered prediction: predictive confidence would be imperfectly calibrated, with calibration differing across the three subjects.

05 / Interpretation & integrity

What the findings can—and cannot—say

Scope: This is a 300-question study of one model and three MMLU subject subsets. It should not be generalized to all language models.

Key interpretation

Predictive confidence consistently exceeded empirical accuracy across populated bins. A model's highest-probability answer can still be incorrect, including in the highest-confidence group.

Sample and benchmark limitations

The sample is small and covers only three domains. MMLU is publicly available and may have appeared in model pretraining data.

Measurement limitations

Softmax-normalized option likelihoods are not guaranteed to be calibrated probabilities. Results depend on the model, prompt, tokenizer, scoring method, answer format, and ECE binning.

Prior test exposure

Results from an earlier version's test evaluation had already been viewed. The corrected analysis is exploratory, not a pristine blind replication. The held-out split was separated from development within the corrected run, but was not completely untouched across project iterations.

Data availability note

The retained aggregate JSON was reconstructed from recorded notebook execution output. It preserves reported aggregate metrics and calibration-bin summaries, but is not a substitute for the original row-level prediction CSV files.
